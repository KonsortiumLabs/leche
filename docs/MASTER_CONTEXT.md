## 1. Executive overview

- **Brand name:** LÈCHE. Exact spelling includes a **grave accent over È**. Do not substitute LÉCHE, LECHE, LÈCHÉ, etc.
- **Instagram:** @tasteleche (in use; six posts visible in the last screenshot provided).
- **Core idea:** an aspirational, sensual, playful, fashion-forward *sensory beauty* brand, starting with a **flavored nail lacquer concept** designed to be tasted if and only if a safe, validated formulation can be developed. The aim is to distinguish it from conventional polish based on color alone or scented beauty products. Future sensory products are possible, but do not crowd the current launch with body glaze, lip gloss, fragrances, etc.
- **Name meaning:** French *lèche* evokes “lick.” Language like “lick me” may be considered for future campaigns, not the master brand name.
- **Positioning:** luxury/cool-girl/celebrity-beauty creative quality with a subtle flirtatious edge; contemporary and Gen Z relevant, but not childish, explicit, fetish-first, medical, or a generic nail-salon brand.
- **Benchmark references:** SKIMS, Kylie Cosmetics, rhode, refined Parisian fashion/beauty editorials. These are art-direction benchmarks only. Do **not** imply affiliation, ownership, celebrity endorsement or partnership.
- **Visual north star:** photographic depth, credible materials and anatomy, intentionally varied compositions, warm skin, red-black/oxblood lacquer, champagne blush, reflective glass, soft glowing light, evocative fruit as an occasional flavor motif. High visual craft is the primary requirement.
- **Current campaign motif:** cherry / “CHERRY 01.” Cherry is a *launch shade/flavor visual motif*, **not** LÈCHE's permanent or exclusive brand identity.

## 3. Product: what it is and what it is not

- **Proposed first product:** wearable colored nail lacquer meant to add a genuine flavor/taste dimension, potentially suitable for adult manicures and pedicures if validated. The differentiation is not just fragrance; the user prefers the concept “not scented. flavored.”
- **Development stage:** concept only. No confirmed finished formula, taste-safety data, shelf-life testing, microbiological stability results, ingredient declaration, regulatory classification, manufacturer, MOQ, unit cost or authorized sale. Do not simulate fake functionality or falsely imply stock.
- **Desirable product properties:** vegan and cruelty-free, naturally inspired sourcing, potentially organic ingredients, but these are **goals, not currently verified product claims**. “Natural,” “organic,” “vegan,” “cruelty-free,” “plant-based,” “10-free,” and “safe to eat” must not be asserted as facts. Never use a certification seal unless legitimately earned.
- **Important safety:** conventional nail polish is not edible. A novel ingestible/taste-safe nail coating may involve cosmetic, food, toxicology, labeling and contamination considerations; have a qualified formulator and regulatory/safety advisor validate it before sales or human tasting. Do not advise users to taste concept renderings or untested homemade product.
- **Packaging direction:** sourceable, stock-style clear-glass nail polish bottle with a substantial but not oversized base, petite-to-proportionate **deep oxblood cylindrical cap**, ivory original Pillow Lava LÈCHE wordmark, refined support lettering. Chrome cap was considered, but user preferred oxblood/ivory cohesion over chrome/cream mismatch.
- **Label hierarchy (concept only):** exact LÈCHE wordmark; “FLAVORED NAIL LACQUER” (or “NAIL LACQUER” in current renders); “CHERRY 01” as *working* shade/flavor; proposed nominal fill e.g. “15 mL / 0.5 fl oz” only after physical bottle capacity is confirmed. Packaging must eventually include correctly researched regulatory labeling and ingredient details. Do not fabricate INCI, company address, safety claims, warnings, certifications or UPC.
- **Bottle creative reference:** `assets/feed/row-2-middle-cherry-01-bottle.jpg`. This is a conceptual render, not proof of availability or final manufactured design.

## 4. Audience, tone and differentiation

- **Target first impression:** “This looks like a tastefully art-directed, culturally interesting celebrity-caliber beauty brand.” Do not claim a Jenner actually owns it.
- **Primary audience:** fashion- and beauty-oriented Gen Z/millennial adults who respond to collectible design, texture, playfulness, personal expression, and a playful sensorial twist.
- **Personality:** chic, tactile, flirtatious, confident, mischievous, female-friendly but not overtly girlish, suggestive but not overtly erotic.
- **Caption tone:** lowercase, sparse, human, memorable, Gen Z without overdone emoji or AI cliché. Usually one to three concise lines.
- **Content differentiation:** create an intriguing visual world, then **clearly explain the intended flavor feature** in product-reveal captions and the landing page. Pure mood without clarity makes viewers think it is ordinary nail polish.
- **Favorite language from prior exploration:** “pleasure looks good on you.” / “a little more than meets the eye.” / “not scented. flavored.” / “good taste gets a little messy.” / “just a little tease.” / “something sweet is coming.” These are tonal references, not all final approved site copy.

## 6. Website objective

**Build a real, polished, mobile-native, conversion-optimized LÈCHE brand site for the concept-validation phase.** Keep it one clear product story, not a sprawling generic cosmetics store. The site needs to feel legitimate and polished **while truthfully communicating that the formulation is in development**.

- **Primary action:** join an actual waitlist / express flavor interest. Email signup must persist and be verifiably functional; do not invent lead counts.
- **Secondary:** learn what makes LÈCHE different; view the flavor-inspired campaign; visit Instagram.
- **Positioning copy:** stylish hero tease plus a *clearly visible* “our first concept: flavored nail lacquer” explanation; preserve a distinct line such as “not scented. flavored.” as a potential campaign kicker.
- **CTA:** “Join the list,” “Be first to know,” or an equally restrained phrase; user has not locked exact CTA.
- **Privacy and trust:** proper consent and privacy link; protect emails, limit collection, prevent spam, and don't misrepresent launch date. No invented reviews, celebrity connections, formulations, lab verification, sold-out labels, stock counts, price, certifications, or functioning checkout.
- **Domain:** `tasteleche.com` has been considered; ownership/availability is **not confirmed in this context**. Do not assert a domain exists or fabricate a live URL.

## 7. Website product-design brief

**Experience:** editorial, mobile-first, immersive *without* needless animations. High contrast between imagery and clean negative space. Use cinematic page transitions only if performant; avoid generic templates, endless feature lists, overdone parallax, scroll-jacking, or unreadable ornamental typography.

**Suggested concise structure:**
1. Landing hero: original LÈCHE wordmark + an editorial image + simple value proposition + primary waitlist action.
2. “The first taste”: explain the proposed flavored nail lacquer concept and CHERRY 01 working campaign. Distinguish taste from scent, explicitly as development goal.
3. Texture/visual story: approved imagery, with moments of soft blush and deep oxblood and tasteful motion.
4. Early access: actual signup with optional one-field flavor preference / poll, clear consent, success and duplicate states.
5. Footer: Instagram, contact method, privacy notice and clear development-status disclaimer.

**Implementation constraints:**
- Use existing approved images as assets; preserve intentional crops and LÈCHE logo rather than recreating imagery with random text.
- Build and test working frontend, backend/storage for leads, responsive breakpoints, keyboard focus states, accessible labels, performance, alt text, OG metadata, robots/sitemap, SEO, no broken links, secure env vars.
- Stack can be chosen based on existing repository. If starting new, a lean TypeScript web app with real persistence is acceptable; prefer simple maintainable implementation over unnecessary libraries.
- Provide a local working preview and verify user flows. Do not claim deployed/live until actual deployment verified.
- If a mailing-list provider, database, or deployment credential is missing, implement a clearly marked integration boundary and list exactly what must be connected. Do not fake a successful signup.
